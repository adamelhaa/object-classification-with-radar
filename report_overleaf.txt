\section{Problem and data}
We classify radar recordings of six daily activities (walking, sitting down,
standing up, picking up an object, drinking, falling) from their micro-Doppler
signatures. The data is the University of Glasgow INSHEP set: 1754 \texttt{.dat}
recordings from an Ancortek FMCW radar (5.8\,GHz carrier, 400\,MHz bandwidth, 1\,ms
sweep, 128 samples/sweep, 1\,kHz PRF) across seven campaigns. Datasets~1--5 are
mostly young adults in labs and offices; datasets~6--7 are elderly people in care
settings (ages up to 98).

Parsing the filenames surfaced three facts that shape the work. (i)~Activity is read
from the \texttt{Ayy} field, the field the provided \texttt{Label\_extract4.m} uses;
the redundant leading digit disagrees with it in three files (typos). (ii)~Subject
IDs restart per campaign, so the same \texttt{Pxx} is a different person in different
datasets; \emph{all} evaluation therefore holds out whole \texttt{(dataset, subject)}
pairs. (iii)~Fall is the minority class (197 vs $\approx$312 per class) and is
essentially absent from the two care sites, so cross-site tests use the five shared
classes. The README datasheet and the MATLAB comment also disagree on the names of
activities~4 and~5; we follow the README (authoritative) and flag it.

\section{Signal processing}
Each recording becomes a velocity-vs-time image, following the provided
\texttt{DataProcessingExample.m}: an FFT along fast time (within a sweep) gives range;
a 4th-order Butterworth high-pass along slow time (MTI) removes static clutter; a
short-time FFT along slow time, summed over the target range bins, gives the
micro-Doppler spectrogram, with $v=f_d\,c/(2 f_c)$. We crop to the human velocity band
($|v|\le 6$\,m/s), resample to a fixed $128\times128$ image so recording-length
differences do not leak in, and normalise to dB referenced to each image's maximum over
a 40\,dB range. The same input feeds both classifiers; preprocessing is run once and
cached. We verified the port against the MATLAB example on a walking file.

\section{Models and evaluation}
We compare two approaches on identical subject-independent splits (GroupKFold on the
\texttt{(dataset, subject)} key). The first is interpretable: 26 physical descriptors
per spectrogram (Doppler centroid and bandwidth trajectories, torso/limb and
approach/recede energy split, temporal envelope) into an RBF SVM. The second is a
ResNet18 fine-tuned on the dB image (first convolution reduced to one channel,
pretrained weights averaged), trained on an Apple-silicon GPU. The CNN's epoch is
selected on an inner subject-held-out split, so the test fold is never used for model
selection.

\section{Results on clean data}
On dataset~1 (balanced, single site) both models reach $\approx\!0.96$
(Table~\ref{tab:res}); the SVM matches the CNN at a fraction of the cost ($\sim$1\,s
vs $\sim$45\,s) and stays fully interpretable. The errors are physical
(Fig.~\ref{fig:cm}): walking is near perfect; the dominant confusion is pick vs drink,
both low-velocity arm motions with similar Doppler extent; the sit/stand mirror pair,
which we expected to be hardest, is largely resolved by the directional (positive vs
negative Doppler) features. On clean single-site data, model capacity is not the
bottleneck.

\section{Generalisation: the headline}
Training on the lab/university pool (datasets~1--5) and testing on the elderly care
sites (datasets~6--7), five-class, accuracy falls from $\approx\!0.89$ within the lab
pool to $\approx\!0.77$ on the care data, and to $0.74$ for West Cumbria
(Fig.~\ref{fig:gen}). The CNN does \emph{not} generalise better than the SVM: averaged
over seeds it lands at the same place and is less stable, so the extra capacity buys no
cross-domain robustness. We measure two causes. First, the elderly perform pick and
drink more slowly and with smaller motions, deepening that already-similar confusion.
Second, the range-bin window fixed on the lab data captures only $0.39$--$0.54$ of the
care-home return (vs $0.68$--$0.75$ in the lab) because those subjects stand closer to
the radar: a geometry shift on top of the age and site shift.

\section{Conclusions and next steps}
Micro-Doppler spectrograms separate these activities well, and on clean data simple
interpretable features match a fine-tuned CNN, so the simpler, cheaper, explainable
model is preferable here. The honest result is the generalisation gap: a model is only
as good as the population and geometry it was trained on. Concrete next steps:
re-centre the range window per site (it currently clips the care return), add a small
amount of in-domain care data (domain adaptation), and report fall separately given its
scarcity at the care sites.
